\section{Transfer Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

The benchmark-level changes in Table~\ref{tab:main-results} show that the
aggregate gains are broadly distributed. Trace-7B improves every benchmark in
the suite. Trace-3B improves 21 of 24; its three regressions are small on
ChartQAPro ($-0.14$) and
TreeBench ($-0.66$), and larger on EvoChart ($-1.68$). The corresponding 7B
changes are positive (+2.24, +2.22, and +7.84), indicating that transfer from
the same task distribution remains model-dependent.

WeMath has the largest gain at both scales (+10.95 at 3B and +10.96 at 7B),
but the improvements are not limited to symbolic mathematics. At 7B they also
include CharXivReason (+7.40), PhyX (+7.73), PuzzleVQA (+5.45), CountBenchQA
(+2.67), and BLINK (+3.26). This spread is consistent with the intended role
of task-level breadth: \trace training exposes one policy to many visual
grammars and executable operations rather than optimizing a single benchmark
format. It does not, by itself, establish which domain or operation causes a
particular gain; controlled mixture ablations remain necessary for that
attribution.

The benchmark patterns also caution against treating one model's solve rate as
an intrinsic task difficulty measure. A solve-rate ordering can identify a
useful competence frontier for a fixed checkpoint and protocol, but that
ordering can change with model scale or family. Curriculum methods built on
\trace should therefore recompute empirical difficulty as the policy changes
rather than assign one universal ordering to heterogeneous task contracts.
