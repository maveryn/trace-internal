\section{Generation and Validation Details}
\label{app:generation}

\subsection{Domain construction profiles}

Prompt wording is composed from scene, task, and optional query templates.
Dynamic labels and values come from the same execution trace as the answer.
The profiles below therefore focus on domain-specific construction and visible
vocabularies rather than repeating common prompt and verifier rules.

\paragraph{Charts.}
Procedural marks, axes, scales, legends, and panels realize Cartesian, radial,
map, flow, table, and composition scenes. Renderers vary chart treatments,
palettes, typography, tick density, mark styling, and compatible report
context without changing the underlying data. Entity, temporal, scientific,
and compact label pools provide readable series, category, panel, and region
names while preserving uniqueness within each figure.

\paragraph{Games.}
Procedural boards, tracks, hands, scores, pieces, enemies, and action states are
combined with curated cards, dice, tokens, and sprite-like assets. Layouts and
skins follow each game's visible grammar rather than a shared generic board.
Coordinates, score displays, status labels, and move cues are derived from the
simulated state, with optional HUD and surface treatments providing controlled
visual variation.

\paragraph{Geometry.}
Analytic constructions generate points, lines, angles, polygons, circles,
solids, coordinate systems, and measurement instruments from solved numeric
constraints. Textbook, whiteboard, and technical-diagram treatments vary
stroke, shading, notation placement, and typography. Point names,
measurements, equations, and option labels are bound after construction so the
visible diagram and symbolic problem statement share one consistent geometric
state.

\paragraph{Graph.}
Graph instances begin with explicit nodes, edges, weights, paths, and state
transitions, then realize them through circular, layered, tree, route, or
force-directed layouts. Node shapes, edge styles, palettes, and framing vary
independently of topology. Numeric, alphabetic, and named node vocabularies are
sampled without collisions, while labels and edge values remain attached to
their semantic graph elements across layout changes.

\paragraph{Icons.}
Curated icon assets populate fields, grids, paths, rings, paired canvases, and
rendered option sets. Procedural controls vary icon type, color, scale,
rotation, spacing, ordering, and transform while maintaining legibility and
answer uniqueness. Short marker and option labels are placed as part of the
scene geometry, allowing counting, adjacency, transformation, and matching
objectives to share a stable visual vocabulary.

\paragraph{Illustrations.}
Semantic object, part, and tile catalogs are composed into rooms, construction
sites, libraries, tactical maps, and isometric scenes. Scene-specific layout
rules govern occupancy, overlap, depth, and permitted object relations;
palette, asset, framing, and surface variants alter appearance afterward.
Visible tile, object, and option labels are used only where the objective needs
explicit reference, leaving other scenes to rely on their illustrated forms.

\paragraph{Pages.}
Structured records are realized through forms, tables, calendars, schedules,
application controls, documents, and infographic components. Page templates
control hierarchy, alignment, chrome, spacing, and typography, while
information-style and context profiles vary presentation density. Field
names, section headings, dates, values, and control labels are generated from
curated assets and remain linked to the underlying document state.

\paragraph{Physics.}
Procedural apparatus, forces, wires, rays, fields, graphs, and measurement
instruments are assembled from explicit physical quantities and component
states. Technical-diagram profiles vary line work, component treatments,
backgrounds, and label placement while retaining the solved configuration.
Quantities, units, directions, and component names are rendered from the same
state used by formula evaluation, comparison, and state-transition programs.

\paragraph{Puzzles.}
Rule-bearing grids, mazes, pipes, matrices, nets, voxel boards, and candidate
sets are sampled with valid solutions and unique completions. Procedural
symbols, board skins, spacing, missing-cell treatments, and option scaffolds
vary the presentation without revealing the hidden rule. Coordinates,
candidate letters, and compact clues are introduced only when required by the
puzzle grammar or answer interface.

\paragraph{Symbolic.}
Notation-specific generators produce clocks, abaci, dice, spinners, musical
staves, logic circuits, formal tables, tapes, and encoded glyph systems.
Symbols and transitions are represented as executable state rather than
decorative text. Fonts, notation cards, palettes, spacing, and layout vary by
scene, while tokens, values, note names, and state labels remain bound to the
formal structure being queried.

\paragraph{Three-dimensional.}
Procedural meshes and object assets are arranged under explicit cameras,
projections, depth relations, occlusion, and surface constraints. Renderers
vary viewpoint, materials, lighting, ground or wall treatments, and framing
without changing the queried scene relation. Object, lane, wall, point, and
candidate labels are placed after projection and checked against the final
image geometry for visibility and separation.

\subsection{Invariant enforcement}

Semantic generation enforces task-specific constraints and answer uniqueness
before rendering. Render-only controls may not alter program operands, task
definitions, answer values, or verifier semantics. Structural realization is
completed before any geometry-bearing verifier payload is projected, and
post-render effects preserve the final canvas coordinate system. Sampled
choices are derived from independent seeded streams and retained in the
execution trace, allowing the semantic instance and its realized image to be
replayed together.
