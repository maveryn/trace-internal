\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

We test whether answer-based RLVR on \trace transfers beyond the procedural
images used for training. The controlled comparison starts from the
Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct checkpoints
\citep{bai2025qwen25vl} and changes only post-training. The final checkpoint at
each scale is compared with its corresponding base model under one frozen
external-evaluation protocol.

\subsection{Training data and reward}

The training set contains 64 instances from each of the 1,000 tasks, for
64,000 prompts in total. Images are embedded in the dataset and capped at
1.28 million pixels. A separate IID set contains two new instances per task
(2,000 total) and is used for monitoring every 100 updates; external
benchmarks are not used for checkpoint selection. Both model scales use the
same task identities, generated instances, prompts, and typed answers.

The model may reason freely, but must end its response with a JSON object of
the form \texttt{\{"answer": ...\}}. Let $R_a$ indicate exact equality after
type-aware answer canonicalization and let $R_f$ indicate a valid terminal
JSON object with exactly the requested key. The bounded reward is
\begin{equation}
  R = 0.95 R_a + 0.05 R_f.
\end{equation}
No image-space annotation reward, learned reward model, or LLM judge is used
during training.

\subsection{Optimization}

We train with group relative policy optimization (GRPO), sampling eight
responses for each of 128 prompts per update. Rewards are normalized within
each prompt group and optimized with a clipped token-level policy objective.
Both models receive 500 updates, exactly one shuffled pass over the 64,000
prompts and 512,000 sampled responses. We use full-parameter training with a
learning rate of $10^{-6}$, one policy epoch, BF16 parameters, and no KL
penalty. Sampling uses temperature 1.0 and top-$p$ 1.0. Training runs on eight
H100 80GB GPUs and takes 12.2 hours for 3B and 13.9 hours for 7B. The complete
shared configuration appears in Appendix~\ref{app:experiments}.

\subsection{External evaluation}

The frozen \texttt{trace\_eval\_v1} suite contains 24 benchmarks organized
into six analysis groups: charts and tables, visual mathematics, science and
general reasoning, spatial reasoning, perception and counting, and puzzles
and logic. We evaluate the full selected split of every benchmark, totaling
32,805 examples per model and decoding seed. Each benchmark retains its
native scoring contract. Category scores are unweighted means of four
benchmark scores, and the overall score is an unweighted mean of all 24, so a
large benchmark cannot dominate the aggregate.

Generation uses temperature 0.6, top-$p$ 1.0, a 4,096-token response cap, and
decoding seeds 42, 43, and 44. These are repeated decoding seeds for one fixed
checkpoint, not independent training runs. We report the mean and sample
standard deviation across seeds. All models use the same benchmark prompts,
image preprocessing bounds, parsers, and scorers under a pinned VLMEvalKit
revision; the full suite is listed in Table~\ref{tab:evaluation-suite}.

For context at 7B, we also evaluate publicly released Qwen2.5-VL checkpoints
from Game-RL, Sphinx, Jigsaw-R1/PCGRPO, and Vero
\citep{tong2025gamerl,alam2026sphinx,wang2025jigsawr1,sarch2026vero}. These
comparisons use the same external protocol, but their training corpora,
optimization methods, and compute budgets differ. They are therefore
contextual baselines rather than controlled alternatives to \trace training.
