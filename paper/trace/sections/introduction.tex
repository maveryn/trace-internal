\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has become an effective
route to stronger reasoning in language models because mathematical and
programmatic tasks admit inexpensive, objective feedback
\citep{shao2024deepseekmath,deepseekai2025deepseekr1}. Extending this recipe to
vision-language models is more difficult. A useful visual example must support
both exact verification and genuine dependence on the image, while also
varying enough in content and presentation to avoid training on a narrow visual
template. Recent multimodal RL methods demonstrate progress on mathematics,
counting, spatial reasoning, and general visual understanding
\citep{liu2025visualrft,huang2025visionr1,peng2025lmmr1,meng2025mmeureka}, but
the data needed to train broadly transferable visual reasoning remains a
central constraint.

Two complementary directions expose this limitation. Large mixtures provide
coverage by combining many existing datasets and task-specific rewards
\citep{sarch2026vero}, but their sampling units and reasoning boundaries are
inherited from heterogeneous sources. Procedural systems provide exact labels
and controlled generation, yet typically concentrate on one visual grammar or
a small family of related objectives, including jigsaws, logic puzzles, games,
and abstract perception tasks
\citep{wang2025jigsawr1,feng2025visualsphinx,tong2025gamerl,
alam2026sphinx,yang2026tron}. Consequently, breadth, controllability, and
verifiability are rarely represented within one coherent task organization.

\trace addresses this gap through a program-centered taxonomy. A scene defines
the visible grammar---its objects, relations, and layout family---whereas a
task defines the reasoning program, answer type, and verifier semantics. A
bounded query changes a meaningful program argument, such as extremum
direction, without creating another task. This distinction extends the use of
structured scene representations and executable question programs in systems
such as CLEVR and Task Me Anything
\citep{johnson2017clevr,zhang2024taskmeanything}: the taxonomy determines not
only generation, but also sampling, verification, and cross-domain analysis.

The resulting environment contains 1,000 tasks across 277 scenes and 11 visual
domains. Each image and answer is derived from the same latent scene state, so
verification evaluates a known program result rather than inferring a label
from pixels. Seeded generation supports deterministic replay, while
scene-compatible controls vary object populations, values, layout, palette,
typography, contextual text, and bounded raster effects. Figure~\ref{fig:domain-montage}
illustrates the resulting visual breadth.

We use this environment to test a direct empirical question: whether
answer-based RLVR over a broad procedural distribution improves reasoning on
external visual benchmarks rather than only on regenerated instances from the
same task families. Training on 64,000 instances improves the 24-benchmark
macro-average by 3.51 points at 3B and 4.06 points at 7B. The 7B model improves
all 24 benchmarks under the matched evaluation protocol, while benchmark-level
results retain the smaller 3B regressions rather than hiding them in the
aggregate.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a program-centered taxonomy that separates visual scene grammar from
        executable reasoning task and bounded query variation across 1,000
        tasks, 277 scenes, and 11 domains;
  \item a deterministic generator--renderer--verifier formulation with typed
        answers, replayable traces, and answer-preserving visual variation; and
  \item a controlled answer-based RLVR study showing broad transfer from this
        procedural distribution across 24 external visual-reasoning benchmarks.
\end{itemize}
