\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The taxonomy is designed and curated by humans, so its task boundaries remain
judgment calls even when guided by program contracts. Procedural renderers can
introduce style regularities unlike natural images, and broad task count does
not guarantee complete coverage of real visual reasoning. Metadata verifiers
guarantee consistency with the generated world, not that every rendering is
equally easy to perceive.

The experiments use one training run per model size; the reported variation
comes from three decoding seeds, not independent post-training runs. External
checkpoints are evaluated consistently but are not matched in data,
optimization, or compute, and several benchmarks retain their native
model-based graders. The 24-benchmark suite is broad but cannot establish
transfer to every real-image distribution. Mixture ablations are also needed
to separate the effect of individual domains from the effect of training on
the complete task distribution.

\trace does not define task-local difficulty ladders or a universal ordering
across tasks. Solve rate depends on the evaluated model and protocol, while
structural factors such as distractor quality in multiple choice and
derivation depth in geometry are not directly commensurate. Future work could
combine contract-preserving complexity axes within tasks with cross-task
prerequisite relations inferred from executable programs and empirical
learning curves. \trace additionally contains projected visual annotations
used during task auditing, but establishing whether and how they should be
used as training targets remains future work.
