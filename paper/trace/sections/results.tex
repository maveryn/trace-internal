Answer-only training improves the benchmark-macro average at both scales. At
3B, \trace raises the score from $39.34\pm0.63$ to $42.85\pm0.39$, a gain of
3.51 percentage points. At 7B, it raises the score from $47.93\pm0.30$ to
$51.99\pm0.17$, a gain of 4.06 points. These are not gains confined to one
analysis group: all six category averages improve at both model sizes.

The largest 3B category gain is on visual mathematics (+7.44), followed by
puzzles and logic (+3.75), science (+3.50), and spatial reasoning (+3.24).
At 7B, the gains are largest for visual mathematics (+5.65), charts and tables
(+5.15), and science (+4.55); the smallest category gain is still +2.25 on
spatial reasoning. Thus, increasing model scale does not eliminate the effect
of the procedural training distribution.

Under the common external protocol, Trace-7B also exceeds Game-RL-7B by 3.94
points, Sphinx-7B by 2.64, and PCGRPO-7B by 3.18. Vero-7B obtains the highest
overall contextual score at 52.22, 0.24 points above Trace-7B. The two models
have different strengths: \trace is highest on charts and tables and narrowly
higher on science, whereas Vero is higher on the other four categories. Since
these checkpoints are not matched in training data or compute, the comparison
characterizes the resulting policies rather than isolating a data effect.
